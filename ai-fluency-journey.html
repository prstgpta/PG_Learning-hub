AI Fluency / Learning Journey
Based on the 4D Framework by Dakan + Feller, in collaboration with Anthropic
A practical path to better human–AI collaboration

Make better decisions with AI.

AI fluency is more than prompting. It is the discipline of choosing the right work, describing it clearly, judging what comes back, and staying accountable for the result.

Four capabilities. One human in the loop.

The leadership view

A compact route for leaders who need to set direction, govern risk, and build a culture of responsible AI use.

01 / FRAME

Set the ambition

Identify where AI can improve quality, speed, learning, or decision support. Start with outcomes, not tools.

02 / ASSIGN

Choose the mode

Decide whether a task needs automation, augmentation, or an independently operating agent.

03 / GOVERN

Build the checks

Define review points for accuracy, privacy, bias, safety, disclosure, and escalation.

04 / LEARN

Measure and adapt

Track what improves, what fails, and where human judgment remains essential.

The 4D lens

Use these four questions in investment decisions, operating reviews, and team rituals.

D1 / Delegation

Should AI do this?

Match the task to the right actor. Protect human ownership of values, judgment, and consequential decisions.

D2 / Description

Have we made the goal clear?

Give AI the audience, context, constraints, workflow, and quality bar it needs to contribute well.

D3 / Discernment

Can we trust the result?

Evaluate outputs and the collaboration process. Require evidence, testing, and visible uncertainty.

D4 / Diligence

Who is accountable?

Humans remain responsible for the final work, its disclosure, and the consequences of deployment.

EffectiveDoes it serve the intended outcome?
EthicalDoes it respect people and principles?
SafeCan we manage failure and harm?

Build the practice

A guided sequence for practitioners, educators, and teams. Work through the modules in order, then apply the capstone to a real workflow.

Outcome

By the end, you should be able to design an AI-assisted workflow that is useful, reviewable, transparent, and aligned with human judgment.

“Decide wisely. Describe clearly. Evaluate critically. Remain responsible.”

01

What AI can and cannot do

Learn the difference between fluent output and reliable knowledge. Map capability, uncertainty, context limits, and tool access.

Practice: Compare an AI answer with trusted sources and mark facts, assumptions, missing context, and uncertainty.

Outcome: A capability map for one AI tool you use.

02

Delegation: define the human role

Break a real task into human-only, automated, collaborative, and approval components. Select automation, augmentation, or agency deliberately.

Practice: Map a workflow and identify where human values, judgment, and final decisions must remain visible.

Outcome: A human-AI responsibility map with review checkpoints.

03

Description: communicate the work

Practice product description, process description, and performance description. Add audience, constraints, examples, and a quality bar.

Practice: Turn a vague request into a collaboration brief with goal, context, format, constraints, and success criteria.

Outcome: A reusable prompt and process description for a real task.

04

Discernment: inspect and iterate

Review outputs for accuracy, completeness, relevance, bias, and risk. Improve the collaboration instead of only rewriting the answer.

Practice: Run a structured review, separate observations from recommendations, and ask the AI to expose weak assumptions.

Outcome: An evaluation rubric and a documented revision loop.

05

Diligence: own the consequences

Build checks for privacy, copyright, disclosure, safety, testing, monitoring, and escalation before anything is shared or deployed.

Practice: Conduct a risk review covering affected people, sensitive data, failure modes, disclosure expectations, and escalation paths.

Outcome: A release checklist and a short AI-use disclosure statement.

06

Capstone: redesign one workflow

Apply the 4D lens to a real task. Document the workflow, risks, checkpoints, and evidence that would make it ready for use.

Practice: Redesign the workflow, test it with representative examples, and collect feedback from its intended users.

Outcome: A responsible AI workflow brief that another person could review and operate.

Capstone worksheet

Use this before opening an AI tool. It turns a vague request into a responsible collaboration brief.

GOAL: What outcome matters?
MODE: Automation, augmentation, or agency?
HUMAN ROLE: What must remain human-owned?
CONTEXT: What does the AI need to know?
QUALITY BAR: How will we judge the result?
RISKS: What could go wrong, and for whom?
CHECKS: What will we verify before release?
  • Task and success criteria are explicit
  • Sensitive information is protected
  • Human approval points are visible
  • Facts and assumptions are separated
  • Failure and escalation are defined
  • AI involvement is disclosed where needed
